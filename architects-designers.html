Why Firms Choose 4C

The case for specifying 4C on your next project.

Architects and designers don't choose a construction system on faith. It has to hold up on five fronts: how much time it saves, what it does to a client's budget, how much of the coordination burden it takes off your desk, whether the manufacturer is actually there when you need them, and whether it makes your firm look better or worse to the client who's trusting you with their project. Here's how a 4C component system holds up on each, and why we designed the process around keeping your design intent intact instead of asking you to compromise it for manufacturability.

Time: parallel paths instead of sequential ones

On a conventional project, permitting, engineering, and procurement often happen in sequence, each waiting on the last to finish before the next can start. Because our configurator converts your design into defined components and specifications early, permit documentation and manufacturing can move in parallel instead of waiting on each other. That shortens the gap between a permitted set and a project actually breaking ground, and it's part of why projects on the 4C platform see onsite construction time cut by up to 30% compared to stick-framing. For a client asking how soon they can move in, that parallel timeline is often the difference between a schedule you can commit to and one you can only estimate.

Cost: no redesign fee, fewer field change orders

Because our configurator adapts your existing design rather than asking you to redraw around a proprietary system, there's no redesign cost eating into your fee or your timeline. And because every component is manufactured to the exact dimensions of your certified, permitted drawings, there are far fewer field changes that quietly erode a design after you've handed it off, the kind of changes that generate change-order conversations you'd rather not be having with a client six weeks into construction.

Convenience: one coordinated model, not a dozen file handoffs

Instead of your drawings disappearing into a black box between design and manufacturing, our configurator and coordinated digital building model keep your design intent attached to every component spec, permit set, and certification document, across both the 4C SAFE® and 4C ECO™ systems. You're not chasing down which version of a file is current across three different consultants; there's one model that reflects the actual building.

Service: engineering support that stays with the project

We generate the permit and construction drawing set as part of our process, provide engineering certification and technical documentation, and stay available for ongoing project support, so the technical heavy lifting doesn't default back to you when a plan checker has a question. That's a genuine extension of your team's bandwidth, not another vendor you have to manage, and it holds whether you're specifying 4C SAFE® for a fire-rated rebuild or 4C ECO™ for a standard new build, since both draw on the same engineering and support team.

Credibility: a stronger pitch, backed by real buildings

When you specify a 4C system, you're handing your client more than a design: you're handing them a documented, ICC-ES listed, patent pending system that 4C carries from that design all the way through manufacturing, delivery, and installation under warranty, with a portfolio of completed projects they can visit in person, including the Borrego Springs installation time-lapse. That's a stronger answer to "how do we know this will actually get installed on time" than a construction schedule you're hoping holds up, and it differentiates your firm from others still pricing conventional stick-framing as the only option.

None of this changes how you practice architecture. You design the way you already do, for the client and site in front of you, whether that's a ground-up single-family home, a fire rebuild, or multifamily infill, and bring it to us at any stage from an early concept sketch to a fully permitted set. We fit into your existing relationships with owners and developers and the builders and GCs who carry the rest of the project, rather than asking any of you to restructure how a project gets delivered. It's also why we can flag early which system, or combination of systems, fits a given construction type and local code requirement, instead of leaving that discovery for later in design when a change is far more expensive to make.
