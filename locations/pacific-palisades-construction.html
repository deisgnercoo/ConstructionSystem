The Palisades Rebuild

Rebuilding the Palisades: hillside terrain, high stakes, and the case for panels.

A closer look at where the Palisades Fire recovery stands, what makes rebuilding here uniquely hard, and why a non-combustible, no-heavy-equipment panel system fits these lots.

Pacific Palisades is rebuilding more slowly than almost anywhere in Los Angeles, and the reasons are specific to this place. The January 2025 Palisades Fire destroyed 6,837 structures across roughly 23,448 acres of coastal hillside and canyon terrain, the most destructive fire in LA history. The first fully rebuilt home didn't receive its Certificate of Occupancy until November 2025, about ten months after the fire, and while hundreds of projects are now under construction, completed homes remain few. The slow pace isn't mainly a permitting problem anymore, it's the terrain and the build.

Permitting moved fast. The terrain didn't.

Los Angeles compressed Palisades permitting dramatically: an executive order mandating 30-day plan review and five-day clearance for like-for-like rebuilds, a self-certification pilot, a Standard Plan program, and a One-Stop Rebuilding Center in West LA, with more than 3,000 rebuild permits approved in the Palisades and debris cleared from the large majority of lots. What that leaves is a physical constraint: steep grades, variable soils, and narrow canyon roads that drive deep foundations, retaining walls, and complex grading while limiting site access and staging room. This is where 4C's approach fits the ground. 4C SAFE® panels arrive pre-labeled and require no heavy equipment to assemble, a real advantage on lots where a crane or laydown yard isn't practical, and they cut onsite construction time by up to 30%. Our construction manufacturing page explains how that off-site process works.

A Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone

The entire Palisades sits within a Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone, so every rebuild must meet California Building Code Chapter 7A wildfire standards, ignition-resistant siding, roofing, vents, and windows, which industry estimates suggest adds on the order of 12 to 18% to construction cost. 4C SAFE® is built for that requirement rather than retrofitted to it: steel-framed, fully non-combustible, and rated 1-HR and 2-HR fire-resistive under ICC-ES listing ESL-1695 (patent pending), giving plan reviewers a clear, code-referenced path for a high fire-risk site. Where a wood-framed approach suits a project better, our 4C ECO™ system is compared side by side on the systems overview.

The underinsurance squeeze

Costs in the Palisades sit at the top of the LA residential market, commonly estimated from the mid-$500s well into the $900s per square foot for custom fire-resilient rebuilds. Meanwhile, carrier rebuild estimates have often come in far lower, leaving many households underinsured by hundreds of thousands of dollars. In that gap, cost and schedule certainty carry real weight. Because 4C manufactures to a fixed, engineered specification, a rebuild is less exposed to the field surprises and change orders that inflate a conventional budget, part of what our four pillars of cost, carbon, complexity, and compliance are built around.
