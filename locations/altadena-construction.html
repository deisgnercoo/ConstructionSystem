The Altadena Rebuild

Altadena's rebuild: why speed and a certified system matter most.

A closer look at where Altadena's Eaton Fire recovery stands, the cost and insurance squeeze households face, and how a proven, fire-resistant panel system fits that reality.

Altadena is rebuilding, but it is early, and the hardest part is now the construction itself. The January 2025 Eaton Fire burned 14,021 acres and destroyed roughly 9,418 structures, including on the order of 6,000 residential properties across Altadena's foothill neighborhoods. About eighteen months on, local recovery data put the numbers at roughly 97 homes fully rebuilt, around 1,785 actively under construction, and more than 3,500 rebuild permit applications submitted, more than half the homes lost. The community is moving, but full recovery will take years, and 4C has already delivered three completed Eaton Fire rebuilds here on the 4C SAFE® system.

Permitting sped up. Construction is now the constraint.

Because Altadena is unincorporated, Los Angeles County handles its rebuild permits, and the County moved aggressively to clear the path: an Altadena One-Stop Permit Center, a Unified Permitting Authority to remove bottlenecks, waived zoning review for like-for-like rebuilds, and a self-certification pilot and pre-approved "Foothill Catalog" plans that trim cost. Permits are now issued around three times faster than the pre-fire average, with some planning approvals cut from months to days. With permitting streamlined, the remaining bottleneck is the build itself: labor, materials, and schedule. That is exactly what panelized construction compresses, cutting onsite construction time by up to 30% because components arrive pre-labeled and sequenced from our Hayward factory rather than being framed board by board on site.

The cost-and-insurance squeeze

Rebuilding here is expensive, and many households are underinsured. Survey data reported for the Eaton burn area puts structure-only rebuild cost near $570 per square foot, with average contractor quotes around $928,000 per home. At the same time, insurer payouts have fallen well short of those figures, leaving many owners short by hundreds of thousands of dollars, which is cited as a leading reason only about half of survivors have applied to rebuild so far. In that squeeze, predictability is worth as much as speed. Because 4C manufactures to a fixed, engineered specification, a rebuild carries fewer of the field surprises and change orders that quietly inflate a conventional budget, which is central to our four pillars of cost, carbon, complexity, and compliance.

ADUs In Rebuild Projects

Adding an ADU while you rebuild.

An Altadena rebuild is often the only practical moment to add an accessory dwelling unit, since the site is already being re-permitted and re-built, so pairing a main residence with an ADU avoids a second disruptive construction phase later. Two of our three completed Altadena projects paired a main residence with a matching ADU; the third was a standalone ADU built on its own. All three used the same 4C SAFE® system, so the ADU carries the same fire-resistant, 1-HR and 2-HR fire-rated construction as the main home.
